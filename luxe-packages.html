Why The Page Count Is The Strategy

You're not paying for more pages. You're paying for more ways to be found.

The jump from $3,500 to $5,500 isn't about polish — every build gets the same craftsmanship. It's about the two page types that actually move you up Google for the searches that book jobs: location pages and project pages. Here's why they're worth it.

Why location pages

One homepage can't rank in ten towns.

When someone in Hinsdale searches "custom home builder" or "kitchen remodel near me," Google looks for a page about Hinsdale — not a homepage that name-drops twelve suburbs in passing. A dedicated location page is a purpose-built front door for one town: local projects, local reviews, and schema that tells Google exactly where you work.

It's the single biggest lever in local search — and the market leaders already pulled it. Today Luxe has zero. Every location page you add is a new shot at being the first result in a town that books six-figure jobs.

Why project pages

Every finished home is a ranking asset you've already paid for.

A dedicated project page — photos, specs, the neighborhood, the client's own words — is original, location-rich content Google can't find anywhere else, and it's the proof that closes your next buyer. Like a landscaper's individual case-study pages, each one earns on its own.

Each page ranks for its own searches ("modern custom home Naperville"), links back to your service and location pages to lift the whole site, and keeps the content fresh without you ever writing a blog. Build it once; it earns for years.
